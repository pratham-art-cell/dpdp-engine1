WhatsApp Medical Reports: Data Leakage Vulnerabilities Under the DPDP Act

Ask any diagnostic lab manager how patient reports are delivered, and most will give the same answer: "We just WhatsApp it to them." While convenient, this practice represents a major hidden legal liability.

1. Endpoint Security Failures on Personal Staff Devices

When a technician downloads a patient's lab report PDF on their personal smartphone and forwards it via WhatsApp, multiple security boundaries are broken. The file sits unencrypted in consumer local storage, often syncing automatically to personal cloud backups. Under Section 8 of the DPDP Act, this lack of rigorous endpoint control constitutes an avoidable security failure.

2. The Risk of Misdirected Dispatches

Front-desk operators handling dozens of calls frequently mix up phone numbers, resulting in confidential pathology results landing in the hands of unintended recipients. Exposing patient diagnostic data to unauthorized third parties triggers mandatory breach notifications.

3. Transitioning to Compliant Tokenized Delivery

Clinics do not need to abandon instant messaging entirely; rather, they must upgrade the underlying delivery mechanism. Instead of attaching raw PDFs, compliant systems transmit secure, time-bound verification links requiring OTP authentication.
