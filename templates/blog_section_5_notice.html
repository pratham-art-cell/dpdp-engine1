DPDP Section 5 Notice Requirements for Pathology Labs & Diagnostic Centers

In diagnostic healthcare, consent has historically been treated as an afterthought—a quick signature scribbled at the bottom of a registration clipboard. Under Section 5 of the DPDP Act 2023, this casual approach is no longer legally defensible.

1. The Mandate for Clear, Itemized Notices

Section 5(1) stipulates that every data fiduciary must provide a notice to the data principal either prior to or at the exact time of collecting personal data. For a pathology lab, this means when a patient registers for a lipid profile or pathology panel, they must be presented with an itemized notice detailing exactly what data is being gathered, why it is necessary, and how it will be stored.

2. Eliminating Coerced and Bundled Consent

A common flaw in legacy diagnostic software is "bundled consent"—wrapping diagnostic processing terms together with marketing permissions or third-party data sharing agreements in dense, unreadable privacy policies. The DPDP Act explicitly invalidates bundled consent. Patients must have the granular option to consent to diagnostic processing without being forced into secondary data monetization or marketing distributions.

3. Multilingual Accessibility (Eighth Schedule)

Compliance requires practical localization. A notice presented exclusively in English to a patient in regional Madhya Pradesh or rural Karnataka fails statutory comprehension tests. Diagnostic centers must ensure their notice architecture supports regional languages specified under the Eighth Schedule of the Constitution, making terms transparent and accessible to every demographic.

4. Operationalizing Notices at the Front Desk

To solve this without creating bottlenecks at peak morning collection hours, labs are moving toward digital intake kiosks and QR-code-enabled consent confirmation links. These systems record exact timestamps and provide patients with immediate digital copies of their privacy notices, satisfying statutory mandates effortlessly.
