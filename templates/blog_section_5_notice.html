Notice Requirements Summary

  • Under Section 5(1), every healthcare provider must serve an explicit, itemized notice before or at the time of collecting patient data.
  • Generic terms on test requisition slips or pre-ticked consent boxes are legally void under Section 6.
  • Notices must be presented in English or any of the 22 scheduled languages under the 8th Schedule of the Indian Constitution.
  • Serving invalid notices exposes clinics to standalone penalties of up to ₹50 Crores under Section 33.

1. The Legal Mandate of Section 5 in Clinical Settings

In standard clinical practice, patient data intake is rapid. Reception desks collect contact details, government identification, and sample identifiers without informing patients of data flows, downstream processors, or storage lifecycles. Under Section 5 of the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, this practice is non-compliant.

As established in our comprehensive DPDP Act Master Healthcare Compliance Guide, clinics are classified as Data Fiduciaries and are statutorily required to provide transparent, unambiguous notice before processing health records.

2. Mandatory Components of a Valid Section 5 Notice

To satisfy DPBI regulatory standards, every notice served at OPD registration counters or diagnostic lab portals must contain five itemized elements:

  • Specific Data Categories: Complete disclosure of demographic details, biometric markers, and diagnostic test profiles collected.
  • Specified Purpose: Clear limitation stating the data is processed solely for conducting tests, issuing reports, and medical billing.
  • Manner of Exercising Rights: Clear instructions explaining how the patient can access their records, request corrections, or withdraw consent.
  • Grievance Officer Credentials: Direct contact details (name, email, phone) of the clinic's designated Grievance Officer or Data Protection Officer.
  • Filing Complaints with the Board: A direct statutory notice informing the individual of their right to lodge a complaint before the Data Protection Board of India.

3. The 8th Schedule Multilingual Requirement

Section 5(3) requires that notices be made available in English and all 22 scheduled Indian languages. If your clinic operates in a non-English speaking demographic, presenting English-only notices renders any resulting consent legally invalid.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

What must be included in a Section 5 Notice for a pathology lab? ↓

A Section 5 Notice must clearly specify the exact personal data collected, the purpose of processing, how data principals can exercise their rights, and details for lodging complaints with the Data Protection Board of India.

Can consent be bundled into a generic test requisition form? ↓

No. Under the DPDP Act 2023, consent must be unbundled, granular, free, specific, informed, and an unambiguous indication of the individual's wishes.
